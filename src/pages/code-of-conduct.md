SAIMI is endorsed by the MICCAI Society and follows the
[MICCAI Society Code of Conduct](https://miccai.org/about-miccai/policies/code-of-conduct-policy/),
adapted below for a one day regional symposium. The standards of behaviour are
the Society's. What differs is who you report to and who decides, because a
SAIMI incident is handled by SAIMI, not by the MICCAI Board.

Two sections have no equivalent in the Society's policy and exist because of how
SAIMI works: the treatment of unpublished work, and event photography.

## Who and what this covers

This code applies to everyone taking part in a SAIMI edition: attendees,
speakers, poster presenters, organisers, volunteers, reviewers, sponsors,
exhibitors, vendors, guests and venue staff.

It covers the symposium day itself, including poster sessions and sponsor
booths; associated social events such as the apéro and the speakers' dinner; any
online or hybrid sessions; and SAIMI's communication channels, including social
media.

## Expected behaviour

- Exercise consideration and respect in your speech and actions.
- Critique ideas, methods and results rather than people.
- Refrain from demeaning, discriminatory or harassing behaviour.
- Be mindful that participants differ widely in seniority, institution,
  background, first language and familiarity with the field. Questions that seem
  basic are welcome, and asking one should carry no cost.
- Respect the names and pronouns people use for themselves.
- Be aware of your surroundings and of fellow participants. Alert an organiser
  to anything dangerous or to any breach of this code.
- Alcohol is served at SAIMI social events. Drink moderately, and take care that
  your behaviour toward others is unaffected.

## Unacceptable behaviour

**Violence and bullying.** Use of physical force, threats that endanger someone's
safety, disruptive behaviour such as shouting, and verbal abuse that creates a
hostile environment.

**Personal harassment.** Conduct with sexual, bigoted, ethnic or racial
undertones, including unwelcome advances, suggestive remarks, unwanted physical
contact, photography without consent, following and stalking.

**Discriminatory or exclusionary comments**, including those relating to gender,
gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size,
race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, and academic seniority or
institution.

**Sustained disruption** of talks, poster sessions or discussions.

**Threats of professional or financial harm**, whether explicit or implied. In a
community this small, where reviewers, supervisors and future employers are in
the same room, this matters more than it might at a large conference.

**Retaliation** against anyone who reports a concern under this code, or who
takes part in handling one.

## Unpublished work

SAIMI is non archival and explicitly invites work in progress. Everything
presented is shared in confidence.

Do not photograph posters or slides, circulate them, or post their content
online without the presenting author's permission. Do not use results you saw at
SAIMI in your own work without asking. If you want a copy of something, ask the
author.

## Photography at the event

Editions may engage a photographer, and organisers may take photographs for the
event website and for social media.

If you would rather not appear in these, tell the registration desk or any
organiser at any point during the day, and we will keep you out of published
photographs. Attendees photographing each other should ask first, and accept a
refusal without discussion.

## Reporting a concern

Two routes, and you may use either:

- **A code of conduct officer.** Each edition names two, at least one of whom is
  not part of the local organising team. They are listed on the edition website
  and in the programme booklet, and are reachable on the day.
- **The steering committee**, at TODO@saimi.ch. Use this route if the concern
  involves an organiser, or if you would rather raise it outside the host
  institution.

If you are in immediate danger, contact venue security or the emergency services
first, then tell an organiser.

You can report during the event or afterwards. You can report an incident that
happened to you, or one you witnessed. Anonymous reports are accepted; they may
limit what can be done in response, but they still inform how future editions
are run.

You may also choose to address the behaviour informally yourself, or to talk it
through with an officer confidentially before deciding whether to make a formal
report. A written report is more useful if it says what happened, when, who was
involved and who else saw it.

## How reports are handled

A code of conduct officer takes the report and decides with the complainant what
happens next.

Where a formal complaint proceeds, the officer will inform the person complained
about, give them the substance of the complaint and a chance to reply in
writing, and speak to anyone else involved or present. Identities are protected
as far as the handling of the complaint allows. The officers may seek outside
advice, including from the host institution.

Officers do not decide outcomes. They put a recommendation to the **steering
committee**, which decides. Any steering committee member involved in the
incident, or with a close working relationship to someone who is, steps out of
that decision.

Urgent safety concerns on the day can be acted on immediately by the organisers,
before any of the above.

## Possible outcomes

The steering committee decides what is proportionate. Available responses
include a private warning, a request to change specific behaviour, a requirement
to leave a session or the event, withdrawal of an accepted abstract or of an
award, and exclusion from future SAIMI editions.

Attendance is free, so there is nothing to refund, but SAIMI does not reimburse
travel or accommodation for anyone asked to leave.

Where conduct may be criminal, SAIMI will cooperate with law enforcement.

## Confidentiality

Reports are shared only with those who need to be involved in responding to
them. Records are kept confidential except where disclosure is required by law
or is necessary to act on the complaint.

## False or malicious complaints

This code exists to protect participants. Knowingly making a false or malicious
complaint is itself a breach of it and is handled the same way as any other.
Reporting something in good faith that turns out to be unfounded is not.

## Appeals

Anyone subject to a decision may appeal in writing to the steering committee
within 30 days. The appeal is considered by members who were not involved in the
original decision.

## Review

The code of conduct officers report to the steering committee after each
edition, noting any incidents in general terms and anything the process got
wrong. The steering committee reviews this document annually.
